### Overview & Origins

**École Polytechnique** (often referred to simply as **"l'X"**) is a prestigious French public institution of higher education and research in science and engineering, located in Palaiseau (Essonne), in the Paris-Saclay innovation cluster.

Founded in 1794 during the French Revolution by the National Convention under the leadership of Lazare Carnot and Gaspard Monge as the *École Centrale des Travaux Publics*, it was renamed *École Polytechnique* in 1795 and militarized by Napoleon Bonaparte in 1804 with the motto:

> *"Pour la Patrie, les Sciences et la Gloire"* (For the Motherland, Science, and Glory).

Historically directed by the Ministry of Armed Forces (formerly Ministry of Defense), École Polytechnique combines rigorous multidisciplinary scientific education with leadership, physical training, and a deep sense of public service.

### Historical Presentation (WebMuseum Archive, 1995)

*The following historical presentation was originally authored in August 1995 by Nicolas Pioch for the WebMuseum (and drawing upon the 1994 Encyclopaedia Britannica), preserved here as part of the academic archives of Promotion X1993.*

#### The Best Students Nationwide
In France, a significant portion of top graduating secondary school students prepare for competitive entrance examinations to the "Grandes Écoles" through intensive two-year preparatory classes (*classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles* or *prépas*). 

Students with a strong background in mathematics and physics take the entrance examination to Polytechnique along with other engineering institutions. Out of several thousand applicants nationwide, a select cohort of approximately 380 French students and international students was admitted annually.

#### Curriculum & Multidisciplinary Formation
- **Initial Training**: French Polytechnique cadets spend their first year in military or civilian service, serving as officers with leadership responsibilities across France and abroad.
- **Academic Core**: Full academic instruction follows with a rigorous core curriculum spanning pure and applied mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, fluid and solid mechanics, computer science, economics, humanities, foreign languages, and athletics.
- **Internships & Research**: Students complete an entry-level operational internship, followed by industrial and laboratory research projects (25% abroad), culminating in advanced research work presented to faculty panels.
- **Post-Graduate Specialization**: The majority of graduates pursue complementary specialized degrees (Ph.D., Master of Science, or engineering state corps schools such as Mines, Ponts, or Télécoms).

#### Campus & Research Facilities
Transferred in 1976 from the historic Latin Quarter (Montagne Sainte-Geneviève in Paris) to a spacious 160-hectare (380-acre) campus in Palaiseau, the school offers cutting-edge laboratory facilities, extensive sports infrastructure (equestrian center, sailing lake, Olympic swimming pools, rugby/soccer pitches), and a historic research library founded in 1794 housing rare scientific collections dating from the 16th century.

#### Campus Life & Traditions
Polytechnique features a vibrant student community managed largely by students through the *Kès* (student union) and numerous associations (*binets*):
- Publications (*X-Passion*, *X-Projets*).
- National and international sporting events (Jumping International de l'X, regattas, inter-school tournaments).
- Cultural activities, theatre, orchestra, and the iconic annual *Point Gamma*.

#### Alumni Legacy & Contributions
Polytechnique alumni have made foundational contributions to world science, industry, and public life:
- **Foundational Scientists**: Augustin Fresnel, Henri Becquerel (Nobel Prize in Physics), Henri Poincaré, Augustin-Louis Cauchy, Paul Lévy, Sadi Carnot.
- **Economists**: Maurice Allais (Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences, 1988).
- **Industrialists & Builders**: André Citroën (Citroën), Auguste Detoeuf (Alsthom), Paul-Louis Merlin & Gaston Gerin (Merlin Gerin).
- **Major National Programs**: Development of the high-speed rail network (TGV), Airbus aeronautics programs, European Ariane launch vehicles, RITA digital telecommunication system, and civil nuclear energy development (CEA).
- **Statesmen**: Presidents of the Republic (Sadi Carnot, Albert Lebrun, Valéry Giscard d'Estaing) and numerous government ministers.
